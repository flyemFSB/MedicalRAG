# F8: Medical / regulated process standards

## Findings

### [1] FDA's final Clinical Decision Support Software guidance (January 2026, docket FDA-2017-D-6569) clarifies which CDS functions are excluded from the device definition by the four Non-Device CDS criteria in section 520(o)(1)(E) of the FD&C Act as amended by section 3060(a) of the 21st Century Cures Act (enacted December 13, 2016).
- quote: "This guidance clarifies FDA's thinking on the types of clinical decision support (CDS) software functions that are excluded from the definition of device by the criteria in section 520(o)(1)(E) of the FD&C Act ('Non-Device CDS criteria')."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01 (final; content current as of 2026-01-29)
- confidence: high

### [2] Software intended to support time-critical decision making fails Non-Device CDS status under FDA's CDS FAQs, because clinicians cannot be expected to independently review the basis of a recommendation under time pressure — directly relevant to any RAG answer surface that might be used in acute workflows.
- quote: "Software that is meant to support time-critical decision making does not meet the definition of Non-Device CDS. Often, time sensitive or critical outputs detect life-threatening and/or time critical conditions. In such situations, it is difficult to support that a clinician is not meant to solely rely on a provided recommendation..."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-29 (content current as of)
- confidence: high

### [3] Software that returns only one clinically appropriate recommendation fails Non-Device CDS Criterion 3 (section 520(o)(1)(E)(ii)); FDA exercises enforcement discretion for simple medical calculations (BMI, mean arterial pressure, Glasgow Coma Scale, NIH Stroke Scale, delivery date estimator) that meet all other criteria.
- quote: "Software that is meant to perform simple medical calculations routinely used in clinical practice is not considered Non-Device CDS software. However, if this software provides only one clinically appropriate recommendation (i.e., would otherwise fail the Criterion 3 in section 520(o)(1)(E)(ii) of the FD&C Act) and still meets all other criteria under section 520(o)(1)(E) of the FD&C Act, FDA intends to exercise enforcement discretion for such software functions."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-29
- confidence: high

### [4] Under FDA CDS FAQs, infrastructure/platform providers (ISPs, general-purpose IT, web hosts) are not device manufacturers, but a developer that provides users access to medical-device software via website subscription or SaaS is considered a device manufacturer — the platform-vs-product distinction matters for how MedicalRAG is marketed and delivered.
- quote: "No, providers of tools, services, or infrastructure used in the development, distribution, or use of software with a medical function are not considered device manufacturers to FDA. ... However, ... a developer that is providing users access to a medical device software through a website subscription, software as a service, or other similar means, is also considered a device manufacturer."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-29
- confidence: high

### [5] IMDRF (chaired by FDA) defines Software as a Medical Device as "software intended to be used for one or more medical purposes that perform these purposes without being part of a hardware medical device"; FDA's SaMD hub lists IMDRF key definitions, risk categorization framework, SaMD QMS, and clinical evaluation as the international baseline artifacts.
- quote: "The term Software as a Medical Device is defined by the International Medical Device Regulators Forum (IMDRF) as 'software intended to be used for one or more medical purposes that perform these purposes without being part of a hardware medical device.'"
- url: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/software-medical-device-samd
- source_type: primary
- published: 2018-12-04 (content current as of)
- confidence: high

### [6] ONC HTI-1 final rule (89 FR 1192, published 2024-01-09, effective 2024-02-08) defines predictive Decision Support Interventions (DSI) as technology that derives relationships from training/example data to produce prediction, classification, recommendation, evaluation, or analysis outputs, and requires intervention risk management (IRM) for every Predictive DSI supplied by a certified health IT developer.
- quote: "we have finalized in § 170.315(b)(11)(vi) that IRM practices must be applied for each Predictive DSI supplied by the health IT developer as part of its Health IT Module."
- url: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/09/2023-28857/health-data-technology-and-interoperability-certification-program-updates-algorithm-transparency-and
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-01-09
- confidence: high

### [7] ONC HTI-1 IRM risk analysis requires Predictive DSIs to be analyzed for potential risks and adverse impacts across eight fixed characteristics adapted from NIST AI RMF: validity, reliability, robustness, fairness, intelligibility, safety, security, and privacy — a concrete checklist usable as a non-certification quality rubric for RAG answer systems.
- quote: "the Predictive DSI must be subject to analysis of potential risks and adverse impacts associated with Predictive DSI the following characteristics: validity, reliability, robustness, fairness, intelligibility, safety, security, and privacy."
- url: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/09/2023-28857/health-data-technology-and-interoperability-certification-program-updates-algorithm-transparency-and
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-01-09
- confidence: high

### [8] ONC HTI-1 IRM is a three-pillar practice (risk analysis, risk mitigation, governance) plus source attributes and ongoing maintenance: developers must maintain policies/controls for how data are acquired, managed, and used; from 2025-01-01 they must review and update source attributes, IRM practices, and public summary information on an ongoing basis; summary IRM info must be publicly reachable via hyperlink with no preconditions.
- quote: "we have finalized § 170.315(b)(11)(vi)(C) requiring that Predictive DSIs must be subject to policies and implemented controls for governance, including how data are acquired, managed, and used... starting January 1, 2025, and on an ongoing basis thereafter, review and update, as necessary, source attribute information... risk management practices... and summary information"
- url: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/01/09/2023-28857/health-data-technology-and-interoperability-certification-program-updates-algorithm-transparency-and
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-01-09
- confidence: high

### [9] FDA/Health Canada/MHRA published 10 Good Machine Learning Practice guiding principles in October 2021; IMDRF released a final GMLP document in January 2025 (N88) elevating those to 10 international principles that address the total product lifecycle of AI/ML medical devices.
- quote: "In January 2025, the International Medical Device Regulators Forum (IMDRF) released a final document that identifies 10 guiding principles that can inform the development of Good Machine Learning Practice (GMLP). ... The final document from IMDRF builds on the guiding principles released in October 2021 by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, Health Canada, and the United Kingdom's Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/good-machine-learning-practice-medical-device-development-guiding-principles
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-12-19 (page content current as of; IMDRF doc Jan 2025)
- confidence: high

### [10] FDA's June 2023 final guidance "Content of Premarket Submissions for Device Software Functions" (docket FDA-2021-D-0775) replaced the 2005 software-content guidance and specifies the documentation FDA expects in premarket submissions for software that meets the device definition — the practical engineering-evidence baseline (software description, architecture, risk management file, verification/validation, unresolved anomalies) even when not claiming certification.
- quote: "This guidance document is intended to provide information regarding the recommended documentation for premarket submissions for FDA's evaluation of the safety and effectiveness of device software functions... This guidance document replaces FDA's Guidance for the Content of Premarket Submissions for Software Contained in Medical Devices issued on May 11, 2005."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/content-premarket-submissions-device-software-functions
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-06 (content current as of 2023-06-14)
- confidence: high

### [11] FDA's February 2026 final cybersecurity guidance (docket FDA-2021-D-1158) supersedes the June 27, 2025 version and ties premarket cybersecurity documentation to section 524B of the FD&C Act for "cyber devices," covering design, labeling, and QMS considerations — security controls are treated as lifecycle obligations, not optional hardening.
- quote: "This document provides FDA's recommendations to industry regarding cybersecurity device design, labeling, and the documentation that FDA recommends be included in premarket submissions for devices with cybersecurity risk. ... This document supersedes the final guidance 'Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality System Considerations and Content of Premarket Submissions,' issued June 27, 2025."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/cybersecurity-medical-devices-quality-system-considerations-and-content-premarket-submissions
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-02 (content current as of 2026-02-03)
- confidence: high

### [12] FDA states its CDS guidance does not expand device oversight and must not be used as the sole reference; products with mixed functions are reviewed function-by-function (device functions vs other functions), and ONC HTI-1 predictive DSIs may or may not meet the FD&C Act device definition — regulatory status is per software function, not per product.
- quote: "FDA recommends ... the CDS guidance is one of several guidances that can help determine whether or not a software function meets the definition of a device and should not be used as the sole reference. ... Thus, it is possible to have a software product with both Non-Device CDS functions as well as device functions that are the focus of FDA's oversight."
- url: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/clinical-decision-support-software-frequently-asked-questions-faqs
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-06-29
- confidence: high
